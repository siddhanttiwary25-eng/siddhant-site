---
title: Everyone knows that everyone knows
date: 2026-10-19
summary: The gap between shared knowledge and common knowledge.
references:
  - David Lewis, Convention (1969)
  - Robert Aumann, “Agreeing to Disagree” (1976)
---

There’s a difference between everyone knowing something and everyone knowing that everyone knows it. The first is shared knowledge. The second, stacked all the way up, is common knowledge, and it changes how people act. Picture a crowd where every person has privately noticed the emperor has no clothes. Nothing happens. Then a child says it out loud. Nobody learned anything new about the emperor, but now each person knows that everyone else knows, and the whole crowd can laugh at once. David Lewis used this idea to explain conventions, like which side of the road we drive on. Robert Aumann showed that two rational people who start from the same prior beliefs, and whose opinions are common knowledge, can’t “agree to disagree”. I keep seeing it in markets and in group chats. A lot of coordination isn’t about information at all. It’s about the moment information becomes public.
